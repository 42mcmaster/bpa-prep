## How to Present Well

### Before the Presentation

#### 1. Know Your Audience
- Research who will be attending
- Understand their knowledge level
- Identify what they care about
- Tailor content to their interests

#### 2. Structure Your Presentation
**Opening (10%)**
- Hook: Start with a question, statistic, or story
- State your purpose clearly
- Preview main points

**Body (80%)**
- Organize into 3-5 main points
- Use clear transitions between sections
- Support claims with evidence
- Use examples and stories

**Closing (10%)**
- Summarize key takeaways
- End with call to action or memorable statement
- Thank the audience

#### 3. Design Effective Slides
**Do:**
- Use large, readable fonts (24pt minimum)
- Limit text: 6 lines per slide, 6 words per line
- Use high-quality images
- Maintain consistent design
- Use contrast for readability

**Don't:**
- Read slides word-for-word
- Use distracting animations
- Overcrowd slides
- Use tiny fonts
- Rely solely on text

#### 4. Practice, Practice, Practice
- Rehearse out loud at least 3 times
- Time yourself
- Practice with your slides
- Anticipate questions
- Record yourself to identify issues

### During the Presentation

#### 1. Body Language
- Stand tall with good posture
- Face the audience, not the screen
- Make eye contact (3-5 seconds per person)
- Use natural hand gestures
- Move purposefully, not nervously
- Smile when appropriate

#### 2. Voice Control
- Project your voice (speak to the back row)
- Vary your tone and pace
- Pause for emphasis
- Avoid filler words ("um," "like," "you know")
- Speak clearly and enunciate

#### 3. Engagement Techniques
- Ask rhetorical questions
- Use stories and examples
- Reference current events
- Incorporate audience participation
- Use humor appropriately

#### 4. Handle Nerves
- Take deep breaths before starting
- Remember: slight nervousness is normal
- Focus on your message, not yourself
- Channel nervous energy into enthusiasm
- Have water available

#### 5. Technical Tips
- Arrive early to test equipment
- Have backup plan (USB, email, etc.)
- Know how to advance slides
- Stand to the side of screen
- Use presenter notes if needed

### After the Presentation

#### 1. Handle Q&A
- Listen to full question before answering
- Repeat question for audience
- Be honest if you don't know
- Keep answers concise
- Stay positive

#### 2. Follow Up
- Thank organizers
- Get feedback
- Share materials if appropriate

## How to Interview Well

### Before the Interview

#### 1. Research
- Study the organization's mission
- Know their products/services
- Understand the position requirements
- Research recent news about the company
- Know the interviewer's name and role

#### 2. Prepare Your Materials
- Update resume (bring multiple copies)
- Prepare portfolio if applicable
- Organize references
- Have pen and notepad
- Bring questions to ask

#### 3. Plan Your Stories
Use STAR method:
- **Situation**: Set the context
- **Task**: Describe your responsibility
- **Action**: Explain what you did
- **Result**: Share the outcome

Prepare stories for:
- Leadership
- Teamwork
- Problem-solving
- Overcoming challenges
- Achievements

#### 4. Practice Common Questions
- "Tell me about yourself"
- "Why do you want this position?"
- "What are your strengths/weaknesses?"
- "Where do you see yourself in 5 years?"
- "Describe a challenge you overcame"
- "Why should we hire you?"

#### 5. Professional Appearance
- Choose appropriate business attire
- Ensure clothes are clean and pressed
- Conservative colors and styles
- Minimal jewelry/accessories
- Well-groomed appearance

### During the Interview

#### 1. First Impression
- Arrive 10-15 minutes early
- Greet everyone professionally
- Firm handshake
- Smile genuinely
- Turn off cell phone

#### 2. Body Language
- Sit up straight
- Lean slightly forward (shows interest)
- Make eye contact
- Don't cross arms
- Mirror interviewer's energy level
- Nod to show understanding

#### 3. Communication Skills
- Listen carefully to questions
- Take a moment to think before answering
- Be specific with examples
- Stay positive about past experiences
- Don't speak negatively about others
- Be honest

#### 4. Answer Strategy
**For behavioral questions:**
- Use STAR method
- Be specific, not vague
- Include measurable results when possible

**For technical questions:**
- Think aloud if appropriate
- Ask clarifying questions
- Admit if you don't know, but show willingness to learn

#### 5. Show Enthusiasm
- Express genuine interest
- Ask thoughtful questions
- Demonstrate knowledge of organization
- Show alignment with values

#### 6. Ask Good Questions
Examples:
- "What does success look like in this role?"
- "What are the biggest challenges facing the team?"
- "How would you describe the culture here?"
- "What opportunities for growth exist?"
- "What are the next steps in the process?"

**Don't ask:**
- Questions about salary/benefits in first interview
- Things easily found on website
- "What does your company do?"

### After the Interview

#### 1. Immediate Follow-Up
- Send thank-you email within 24 hours
- Mention specific discussion points
- Reiterate interest
- Keep it brief and professional

#### 2. Reflection
- Note what went well
- Identify areas for improvement
- Remember details for next round

## How to Speak Publicly Well

### Fundamentals of Public Speaking

#### 1. Overcome Fear
**Strategies:**
- Prepare thoroughly
- Practice repeatedly
- Visualize success
- Start with smaller audiences
- Focus on message, not self
- Remember: audience wants you to succeed

**Physical techniques:**
- Deep breathing (4 counts in, 4 out)
- Progressive muscle relaxation
- Power poses before speaking
- Arrive early to get comfortable

#### 2. Know Your Purpose
Every speech should have a clear goal:
- Inform (teach something new)
- Persuade (change minds or behavior)
- Inspire (motivate action)
- Entertain (engage and delight)

#### 3. Craft Strong Content

**Opening (Capture Attention)**
- Surprising statistic
- Provocative question
- Powerful story
- Bold statement
- Relevant quote

**Middle (Deliver Value)**
- Organize logically
- Use rule of three
- Include evidence
- Tell stories
- Use concrete examples

**Closing (Leave Impact)**
- Summarize key points
- Call to action
- Full-circle back to opening
- Memorable quote or statement

#### 4. Vocal Variety
- **Volume**: Loud enough to be heard; vary for emphasis
- **Pace**: Slow down for important points; speed up for excitement
- **Pitch**: Vary tone; avoid monotone
- **Pause**: Use silence for dramatic effect
- **Emphasis**: Stress key words

#### 5. Physical Presence
**Posture:**
- Stand tall
- Feet shoulder-width apart
- Weight evenly distributed
- Chest open

**Gestures:**
- Use naturally
- Keep above waist
- Match words
- Avoid repetitive movements

**Movement:**
- Move with purpose
- Take stage confidently
- Don't pace nervously
- Use space to emphasize transitions

**Eye Contact:**
- 3-5 seconds per person
- Scan entire room
- Connect with individuals
- Don't stare at floor, ceiling, or notes

#### 6. Use of Notes
- Note cards with key points only
- Large font, easy to read
- Number cards in case dropped
- Don't read verbatim
- Glance, don't stare

#### 7. Handle the Unexpected
- Tech failures: Have backup plan
- Questions you can't answer: Be honest
- Losing your place: Pause, breathe, continue
- Audience distractions: Address if necessary
- Time running short: Skip to conclusion

## Extemporaneous Speech Tips

### What is Extemporaneous Speaking?
Brief prepared speech (typically 5-7 minutes) on a topic chosen shortly before (usually 30 minutes prep time).

### Preparation Phase (30 minutes)

#### 1. Choose Your Topic (2 minutes)
- Pick what you know most about
- Select one you can support with examples
- Choose one with clear stance

#### 2. Brainstorm and Outline (8 minutes)
**Quick outline structure:**
```
I. Introduction
   A. Attention-getter
   B. Thesis statement
   C. Preview of points

II. Point 1
    A. Main idea
    B. Evidence/Example
    C. Analysis

III. Point 2
     A. Main idea
     B. Evidence/Example
     C. Analysis

IV. Point 3
    A. Main idea
    B. Evidence/Example
    C. Analysis

V. Conclusion
   A. Summary
   B. Call to action/Final thought
```

#### 3. Gather Evidence (10 minutes)
- Current events examples
- Statistics (if you know them)
- Personal experiences
- Historical examples
- Expert opinions

#### 4. Create Note Cards (5 minutes)
- Write clearly and large
- Key words only
- Include: intro hook, main points, conclusion
- Number your cards

#### 5. Practice Out Loud (5 minutes)
- Say speech at least twice
- Time yourself
- Adjust as needed
- Don't memorize word-for-word

### Delivery Phase

#### 1. Strong Introduction
- Hook audience immediately
- Clearly state your position
- Preview your main points
- Be confident from the start

#### 2. Clear Organization
- Use transitions: "First," "Additionally," "Furthermore"
- Signpost: "My next point is..."
- Connect ideas logically

#### 3. Support Your Points
- Use specific examples
- Reference current events
- Tell brief stories
- Cite sources when possible

#### 4. Powerful Conclusion
- Don't introduce new information
- Summarize key points
- End with memorable statement
- Leave lasting impression

#### 5. Time Management
- Introduction: 1 minute
- Body (3 points): 4-5 minutes
- Conclusion: 1 minute
- Leave buffer for unexpected

### BPA Extemporaneous Speech Format

**Typical Setup:**
- 30 minutes preparation time
- 5-7 minute speech
- Choose from 3 topic options
- May use note cards
- Judged on content and delivery

**Scoring Criteria:**
- Topic development
- Organization
- Supporting material
- Voice
- Body language
- Eye contact
- Overall effectiveness

### Quick Tips for Success

1. **Stay Current**: Read news regularly
2. **Practice Structure**: Use same format repeatedly
3. **Develop Examples**: Keep mental file of good examples
4. **Build Confidence**: Practice regularly
5. **Time Yourself**: Always practice with timer
6. **Use Cards Minimally**: Glance, don't read
7. **Be Enthusiastic**: Show passion for topic
8. **Make Eye Contact**: Connect with judges
9. **Watch Fillers**: Eliminate "um," "uh," "like"
10. **Finish Strong**: Last impression matters

### Common Topics for Practice
- Technology and privacy
- Social media impact
- Education reform
- Environmental issues
- Leadership qualities
- Entrepreneurship
- Work-life balance
- Global issues
- Ethics in business
- Innovation and change

## General Tips for All Formats

### Mental Preparation
- Visualize success
- Use positive self-talk
- Focus on helping audience
- Remember you're the expert on your topic

### Physical Preparation
- Get good sleep beforehand
- Eat light meal before
- Stay hydrated
- Dress comfortably and professionally
- Arrive early

### Continuous Improvement
- Record yourself practicing
- Seek constructive feedback
- Watch great speakers
- Join speaking clubs (Toastmasters, debate)
- Practice regularly

### Remember
- Everyone gets nervous
- Preparation builds confidence
- Practice makes improvement
- Authenticity connects with audiences
- You've got this!

---

**The key to all forms of public communication is preparation, practice, and presence. Master these skills through consistent effort, and you'll stand out in any BPA competition!**
